POSTPARTUM HEMORRHAGE (PPH)
PPH is defined as blood loss of 500 mL or more within 24 hours after birth. It is the leading cause of maternal mortality globally.
Danger signs: heavy vaginal bleeding after delivery, soaked pads, blood clots, pallor, rapid weak pulse, low blood pressure, confusion.
Immediate actions: massage uterine fundus, give oxytocin 10 IU IM immediately, establish IV access, give IV fluids, do not leave patient alone, call for emergency transport, notify receiving facility.
Referral: refer immediately to secondary or tertiary facility with blood transfusion capacity.
Risk factors: prolonged labour, multiple pregnancy, grand multiparity, uterine fibroids, previous PPH, placenta previa.

ECLAMPSIA
Eclampsia is the occurrence of convulsions in a woman with preeclampsia. It is a life-threatening emergency.
Danger signs: convulsions/seizures/fits in a pregnant or recently delivered woman, loss of consciousness, severe headache, visual disturbances, epigastric pain, hypertension (BP ≥160/110 mmHg).
Immediate actions: protect airway, place in left lateral position, give magnesium sulphate (loading dose 4g IV over 15 min, then 1g/hr maintenance), give antihypertensives if BP ≥160/110, do not leave patient, call emergency transport.
Referral: refer immediately to hospital with ICU capacity.

PREECLAMPSIA
Preeclampsia is hypertension (BP ≥140/90 mmHg) with proteinuria after 20 weeks gestation.
Danger signs: severe headache, blurred vision, seeing spots/flashing lights, swelling of face and hands, epigastric pain, BP ≥140/90 mmHg, reduced fetal movement.
Immediate actions: check BP every 15 minutes, check urine for protein, give antihypertensives if BP ≥160/110, prepare for possible referral, monitor fetal heart rate.
Referral: refer to secondary facility for monitoring and management. Refer immediately if severe features present.

OBSTRUCTED LABOUR
Obstructed labour occurs when the fetus cannot descend through the pelvis despite strong uterine contractions.
Danger signs: prolonged labour (>12 hours active phase), failure to progress, caput succedaneum, moulding, maternal exhaustion, fetal distress, abnormal fetal heart rate (<100 or >180 bpm).
Immediate actions: do not perform fundal pressure, establish IV access, give IV fluids, empty bladder via catheter, call emergency transport immediately.
Referral: refer immediately to facility with surgical capacity for caesarean section.

NEONATAL SEPSIS
Neonatal sepsis is a life-threatening infection in newborns, most common in first 28 days of life.
Danger signs: fever (temperature >38°C) or hypothermia (<36°C), not feeding or poor feeding, lethargy or reduced activity, fast breathing (>60 breaths/min), grunting, chest indrawing, bulging fontanelle, jaundice in first 24 hours, convulsions, umbilical redness or discharge.
Immediate actions: keep baby warm, encourage breastfeeding if able, give first dose of antibiotics (ampicillin + gentamicin) if available and trained, refer immediately.
Referral: refer to hospital with neonatal unit immediately. Do not delay for feeding.

NEONATAL BIRTH ASPHYXIA
Birth asphyxia is failure to establish breathing at birth.
Danger signs: baby not breathing or gasping at birth, blue or pale colour, limp muscle tone, heart rate <100 bpm.
Immediate actions: dry and stimulate baby, clear airway, begin bag-mask ventilation if not breathing after 30 seconds of stimulation, call for help.
Referral: refer to neonatal unit if not responding to resuscitation within 10 minutes.

ANTEPARTUM HEMORRHAGE (APH)
APH is bleeding from the genital tract after 22 weeks of pregnancy.
Danger signs: vaginal bleeding in pregnancy, abdominal pain with bleeding (placental abruption), painless bleeding (placenta previa), tender rigid uterus, fetal distress.
Immediate actions: do not perform vaginal examination, establish IV access, give IV fluids, monitor fetal heart rate, call emergency transport.
Referral: refer immediately to hospital with surgical and blood transfusion capacity.

PUERPERAL SEPSIS
Puerperal sepsis is infection of the genital tract following childbirth or abortion.
Danger signs: fever >38°C within 10 days of delivery, offensive vaginal discharge, uterine tenderness, lower abdominal pain, general malaise.
Immediate actions: give antibiotics (ampicillin + metronidazole if available), ensure adequate hydration, refer.
Referral: refer to secondary facility for IV antibiotics and monitoring.

RUPTURED UTERUS
Uterine rupture is a catastrophic obstetric emergency with high maternal and fetal mortality.
Danger signs: sudden severe abdominal pain, cessation of contractions, fetal parts easily palpable outside uterus, maternal shock, vaginal bleeding, loss of fetal heart sounds.
Immediate actions: establish IV access immediately, give IV fluids rapidly, call emergency transport, do not delay referral.
Referral: refer immediately to surgical facility. This is a surgical emergency.

MATERNAL DANGER SIGNS — GENERAL
Any pregnant or recently delivered woman with the following requires urgent referral:
- Severe headache not relieved by paracetamol
- Visual disturbances (blurred vision, seeing spots)
- Difficulty breathing or fast breathing
- Chest pain
- Severe abdominal pain
- High fever (>38°C)
- Convulsions or loss of consciousness
- Heavy vaginal bleeding
- Foul-smelling vaginal discharge
- Inability to walk or stand
- Reduced or absent fetal movement
